Check your tender requirements before you go to market

Requirement Quality Review reads your RFQ, RFT or Statement of Requirements and lists every requirement in it. It scores each one and flags the ones likely to confuse suppliers or shut out small businesses, so you can fix them before release.

How to use it

  1. Sign in to Claude. If you don't have an account, create a free one at claude.ai when the reviewer asks.
  2. Add your document. Choose a PDF or Word file, or paste the text. You can add an RFQ or RFT with its SOR to catch conflicts between them.
  3. Describe the purchase. Say what you're buying, its total value including GST, and how risky it is.
  4. Start the review. The page tells you how long it will take. When it finishes, copy or download the report.

What it checks

  • ClarityCan a supplier tell exactly what to do?
  • ConsistencyDo any requirements contradict each other?
  • DuplicationIs the same thing asked twice?
  • ProportionalityIs the evidence asked for justified by the value and risk?
  • SME accessWould a capable small business or new entrant be shut out?
  • Defined termsAre acronyms and procurement terms explained?

Questions

Does it cost anything?

No. It runs on your own Claude account, so each review counts towards your plan's usage. A short RFQ needs a few Claude messages. A long SOR can need 20 or more, which may use up a free plan's allowance for the day. If you hit the limit, leave the page open and continue later; finished steps aren't repeated.

What happens to my document?

Your file is read in your browser, and its text is sent to Claude under your own account. Only review documents that are ready for release to market. Don't add anything classified above OFFICIAL.

Can I rely on the results?

Treat them as a second pair of eyes. Each issue says whether it came from a fixed rule check or from Claude's judgement, and quotes the requirement so you can check it against your document. The proportionality tier follows the Commonwealth Procurement Rules threshold of $80,000 and can be argued either way.

Who made this?

It's a prototype built on the open-source Wayfinder procurement evaluation project.
